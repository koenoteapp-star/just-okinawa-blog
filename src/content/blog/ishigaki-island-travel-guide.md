Ishigaki Island sits at the southwestern edge of Japan — closer to Taiwan than to Tokyo — and it feels like it.

The pace is different here. The light is different. The water is a shade of blue that doesn't quite exist anywhere else.

This is a guide for people who want to do Ishigaki properly: not just the famous spots, but the ones worth the extra effort.

---

## Getting to Ishigaki

**By plane:** Ishigaki Airport (ISG) has direct flights from Tokyo (Haneda/Narita), Osaka, and Naha. Flight time from Tokyo is around 3.5 hours; from Naha, about 50 minutes.

**From Okinawa Main Island:** The fastest option is a direct flight from Naha. Ferry connections exist but take 10–12 hours — worth considering only if you're not in a hurry.

---

## Getting Around Ishigaki

Ishigaki Island is not walkable from the city. To explore properly, you need wheels.

- **Rental car:** The best option. Roads are easy, parking is free almost everywhere, and you can reach the northern beaches and viewpoints that buses don't serve.
- **Scooter:** A good alternative for solo travellers. Cheaper than a car and easier to park.
- **Bus:** Covers the main tourist spots but runs infrequently. Fine for Kabira Bay; not ideal for exploring freely.

Rental cars book out fast in summer. Reserve before you arrive.

## The Best Beaches in Ishigaki

### Kabira Bay

The postcard image of Ishigaki. Pearl-white sandbars, emerald water, forested hills behind. Swimming is not permitted here — the currents are strong and the bay is protected — but the view from the shore is worth the visit.

Glass-bottom boat tours run from the beach and take you over the coral without getting wet.

👉 [Book a Kabira Bay glass-bottom boat tour](https://www.viator.com/ja-JP/Okinawa/d5614-ttd?localeSwitch=1&pid=P00321249)

### Yonehara Beach

The best snorkelling beach on Ishigaki. The reef starts close to shore, the coral is healthy, and sea turtles are a regular sighting. Snorkel rental is available on the beach.

Go early. By midday it fills up, and the fish seem to know it.

### Sunset Beach

A long stretch of sand on the western coast, facing the East China Sea. The name is accurate — come in the evening and stay until the light is gone.

Less visited than Kabira, more peaceful than Yonehara.

## Things to Do in Ishigaki

### Hike Nosoko Maape

A trail most people drive past without noticing. It starts at the base of a hill in the north of the island and climbs through roots and rocks to a summit with views across the sugarcane fields, the reef, and the sea.

No crowds. No signs pointing you here. One of the best hikes in Okinawa.

→ [Read the full Nosoko Maape hike guide](/blog/nosoko-maape-hike-ishigaki-ep029/)

### Take a Day Trip to Taketomi Island

Taketomi is 10 minutes by ferry from Ishigaki port. Water buffalo carts through coral-walled lanes, red-tiled roofs, bougainvillea everywhere. It looks like Okinawa did 100 years ago.

Ferries run frequently. Go in the morning, before the tour groups arrive.

### Visit Uganzaki Lighthouse

On the western cliff edge of Ishigaki, Uganzaki Lighthouse sits above a coastline of black rock and open sea. The sunset here is the kind you remember.

→ [Read about Uganzaki at sunset](/blog/uganzaki-lighthouse-ep025/)

### Kayak the Mangroves

The mangrove forests around Miyara River are one of the more unexpected things about Ishigaki. Guided kayak tours take you through narrow channels into a landscape that feels completely separate from the beaches and sea.

👉 [Browse Ishigaki outdoor tours](https://www.viator.com/ja-JP/Okinawa/d5614-ttd?localeSwitch=1&pid=P00321249)

## Where to Eat in Ishigaki

### Soba

Okinawan soba is different from mainland ramen — a thicker wheat noodle in a pork and bonito broth, topped with slow-cooked pork ribs. Ishigaki has its own version, slightly lighter, often served with soki (pork ribs) or raft-beef.

Look for small, local shops rather than tourist-facing restaurants. The best ones have no English menus.

### Yaeyama Beef

Ishigaki beef (石垣牛) is some of the finest wagyu in Japan. It doesn't get the same attention as Kobe or Matsusaka, but the locals know. A yakiniku dinner here — beef grilled over charcoal — is worth building an evening around.

### Local Markets

The Ishigaki Public Market (石垣市公設市場) in the city centre is worth a walk through for the fish, the prepared foods, and the general atmosphere of a market that exists for locals, not tourists.

## When to Visit Ishigaki

| Season | What to expect |
|---|---|
| **May – June** | Warm water, some rain, fewer tourists |
| **July – August** | Peak season. Hot, busy, expensive |
| **September – October** | Quieter, water still warm, typhoon risk |
| **November – March** | Cool, very few visitors, some businesses close |

Ishigaki in December and January is quieter than almost anywhere else in Japan and still warm enough for hiking and sightseeing. The beaches are cold for swimming but beautiful to walk.

## How Many Days Do You Need?

- **2 days:** City, Kabira Bay, one beach. A starting point.
- **3–4 days:** Add Taketomi, Yonehara, Nosoko Maape. The right amount of time.
- **5+ days:** Add a day trip to Iriomote or Kohama. You won't want to leave.

---

## Practical Notes

- **Currency:** Cash is essential. Many restaurants and smaller shops don't accept cards.
- **Language:** English is limited outside of hotels and big tourist spots. A translation app helps.
- **Accommodation:** Books out fast in summer. Reserve early.
- **Water:** Tap water is safe to drink.
