The colour has a name. *Kerama Blue.*

It's the shade of the water around the Kerama Islands — a group of small islands about 40 kilometres west of Naha — and it's the reason people come here instead of anywhere else.

If you've seen photos of Okinawa's beaches and thought the colour looked too good to be real, the Kerama Islands are where those photos were taken.

---

## What Are the Kerama Islands?

The Kerama Islands (慶良間諸島) are a cluster of around 20 islands, four of which are inhabited: **Zamami**, **Aka**, **Tokashiki**, and **Geruma**. They sit inside Keramashoto National Park — one of Japan's newest — and the water visibility here can reach 50 metres on a calm day.

This isn't a resort area. There are no big hotels, no poolside bars, no crowds. What there is: some of the clearest water in Japan, coral reefs close to shore, and beaches that feel like they belong to you.

---

## The Best Beaches in the Kerama Islands

### Furuzamami Beach — Zamami Island

The one most visitors come for. White sand, shallow water that shifts from pale turquoise to deep blue, and a coral reef you can reach without a boat.

Sea turtles feed in the seagrass beds just offshore. If you swim out slowly and don't disturb them, there's a good chance you'll share the water with one.

**Getting there:** 15-minute walk from Zamami port, or rent a bicycle.

---

### Aharen Beach — Tokashiki Island

Longer and wider than Furuzamami, with a more laid-back atmosphere. Snorkel rental is available on the beach. The northern end of the reef is less visited and worth the extra swim.

Tokashiki is the largest of the inhabited islands and has more accommodation options if you want to stay overnight.

**Getting there:** 15-minute bus ride from Tokashiki port (runs to match the ferry schedule).

---

### Nishibama Beach — Aka Island

A quieter choice. Aka Island sees fewer day-trippers than Zamami, and Nishibama has the kind of calm that's hard to find in peak season.

The reef here is healthy, and the beach fills slowly even in summer. Worth the extra effort to get here.

**Getting there:** 20-minute walk or bicycle from Aka port.

---

## How to Get to the Kerama Islands

Ferries depart from **Tomari Port** in Naha.

| Route | Ferry type | Journey time |
|---|---|---|
| Naha → Zamami | High-speed | ~50 minutes |
| Naha → Zamami | Slow ferry | ~2 hours |
| Naha → Tokashiki | High-speed | ~35 minutes |
| Naha → Tokashiki | Slow ferry | ~70 minutes |
| Naha → Aka | High-speed | ~60 minutes |

Ferries run daily, but schedules change by season. Book in advance during July and August — they sell out.

---

## Day Trip or Overnight?

**Day trip:** Possible from Naha, but you'll spend roughly 3 hours travelling. That leaves 4–5 hours on the island if you take the morning ferry and the last one back. It's enough to see the beach, but not enough to feel it.

**Overnight:** The better option. Staying even one night lets you see the beach before the day-trippers arrive and after they leave. Both Zamami and Tokashiki have guesthouses and small hotels.

## Snorkelling and Diving

The reef around the Kerama Islands is some of the best accessible snorkelling in Japan. Sea turtles, manta rays (seasonal), and coral that's remained largely intact.

## When to Go

| Season | Conditions |
|---|---|
| **May – June** | Warm water, fewer crowds, some rain |
| **July – August** | Peak season. Busiest, hottest, most expensive |
| **September – October** | Quieter, water still warm, typhoon risk |
| **November – April** | Cool, very few visitors, some services close |

The Kerama Islands are a summer destination. Outside of May to October, some ferry routes reduce frequency and accommodation options narrow. That said, a November visit — if the weather holds — can feel like the islands belong entirely to you.

---

## Practical Notes

- **Cash:** Bring enough from Naha. ATM options on the islands are limited.
- **Sunscreen:** Use reef-safe sunscreen. The coral here is worth protecting.
- **Accommodation:** Book early for July–August. Some places stop taking bookings weeks in advance.
- **Bicycles:** The main way to get around on Zamami and Aka. Rentals are available near the port.
