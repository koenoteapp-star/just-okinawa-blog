What a day of eating in Okinawa looks like.

Morning or night, it always hits.

---

## The Morning

It starts in the car. A toasted bread with egg and cheese, picked up on a drive somewhere between nowhere and the next destination.

It's not fancy. It's exactly right.

## The Night

Onnason, on the west coast of Okinawa's main island. A full course — small plates to start, lobster, Okinawan pork, rice at the end.

The kind of meal where you lose track of time. The kind of restaurant you find once and never forget.

## The Next Morning

Kin Town. ASBO STAY HOTEL. A breakfast box delivered to the room — sandwich, salad, fruit, soup.

You eat it slowly. There's nowhere to be.

## Why Okinawa Eats Different

It's not one thing. It's the range — from convenience-store-level roadside food that somehow tastes better than it should, to courses that hold up against anywhere in Japan.

One day. Multiple places. All of it worth it.
