## The Part Most People Skip

You've already seen what Asumui looks like from the top — the view that opens up, the ocean on both sides, the feeling of standing above the whole island.

This is the other half.

The forest you walk through to get there. Ancient limestone karst formations rising out of the ground, covered in moss and roots. Banyan trees that have been here longer than anyone can say. A canopy dense enough that the light comes through in pieces.

Most visitors move quickly through it. There's a view waiting. There's somewhere to get to.

But the forest is the point.

## What Asumui Is

Asumui is a sacred place in the far north of Okinawa's main island, in Kunigami.

The name itself is Ryukyuan — "the forest that breathes." The landscape is a mix of ancient limestone karst and subtropical jungle that developed together over millions of years. The rock formations are jagged and close, the kind that feel like they belong in a different era.

It's been protected for a long time, and it shows. There are no crowds. The trails are maintained but not smoothed over. Things grow where they want to.

## Getting There

Asumui is about 2 hours north of Naha by car. The road thins out as you get close — that's how you know you're on the right track.

Entry fee applies. Arrive early if you want the forest to yourself.
