The flags go up before the sun goes down.

By nightfall, you can't see where the village ends and the festival begins.

---

## The Hatagai

Before the dancing starts, the flags appear.

Hatagai — tall poles rising above the rooftops, each one carrying the banners, colors, and decorations of its village. No two are exactly alike. Each one takes months to prepare.

They don't move much. They don't have to.

## A Different Kind of Scale

You've seen Hōnen Matsuri from the dancer's side, and from up close.

This is the other view — the crowd, the light, the flags filling the sky as the neighborhood fills the street. Not hundreds of people performing. Hundreds of people being somewhere together.

## Each Village, Its Own Night

Across Ishigaki Island, every village holds its own Hōnen Matsuri. Same festival, different streets, different flags, different night.

This one belongs to one neighborhood.

Walk a few blocks in any direction and you'd find a different crowd, different colors, a different set of flags reaching up into the same sky.

## The Short Version

The dancing gets most of the attention.

But the flags were there first.
