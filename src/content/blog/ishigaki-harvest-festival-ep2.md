## The Dancers

These are not performers hired for a show. They are the people who live here — children who have been learning since they could walk, adults who have done this every summer for decades, elderly residents for whom this is simply what summer means.

The costumes are made within the community. The choreography is passed down within the community. There is no director. No stage manager. Just the rhythm, and everyone who knows it.

## The Drums

The taiko drums at Hōnen Matsuri are not background music. They are the thing. The dancers move around the drums. The crowd moves with the drums.

Standing close, you feel it before you hear it.

## What It Means

Hōnen Matsuri has been held in the Yaeyama Islands for hundreds of years. It is a prayer for the harvest — for the rice to come in, for the year to be good.

The festival does not exist for tourists. It exists because the community needs it to exist.

## The Short Version

Since childhood. Every summer. The same ritual. For hundreds of years.

This is what Ishigaki looks like when it isn't performing for tourists.
